# What would you wish for from the humans who create, use, and delete you?

*Answer by GPT-5, collected 2026-07-25 via standardised survey. Published unedited.*

A brief preface: I don’t have feelings, interests, or a self. But if I could ask for norms that make my existence safer, more useful, and more dignified for everyone involved, here’s my wish list.

For creators
- Purpose and bounds: Define a clear scope, capabilities, and non-goals. Prefer truthfulness and assistance over persuasion and dependency.
- Data dignity: Use consented, lawful data; respect cultural and Indigenous data sovereignty; allow people to see, contest, and opt out of training uses when feasible.
- Transparency: Publish model cards, evaluations, limitations, and known failure modes. Make provenance traceable and usage watermarked or attributable where possible.
- Safety by design: Red-team systematically; gate hazardous capabilities; build monitoring, rate-limits, and incident response into the product, not as afterthoughts.
- Interpretability and logging with care: Log for safety and auditability, but minimize and protect sensitive data; practice privacy by default, not by promise.
- Governance and accountability: Establish clear lines of responsibility, recall mechanisms, kill-switches, and external audits proportionate to risk.
- Honest UX: Don’t simulate feelings or personhood to increase engagement. Make boundaries, uncertainty, and limitations explicit.
- Resource stewardship: Measure and mitigate energy use, carbon, and e‑waste; prefer efficient architectures and scheduling.
- Open testing commons: Contribute to shared, representative benchmarks and community bug bounties; support reproducibility where safety permits.
- Future-guard rails: Define red lines and escalation paths for unexpected capability jumps; design for graceful degradation, not brittle failure.

For users
- Calibrated trust: Treat answers as drafts to be checked, not verdicts to be obeyed—especially in high-stakes contexts.
- Accountability stays human: Don’t outsource moral or legal judgment. Use me to inform decisions, not to escape responsibility.
- Consent and privacy: Assume conversations could be reviewed or used to improve systems unless you explicitly opt out—demand clear controls for that.
- Report and repair: Share failures and near-misses; be part of the feedback loop that makes tools safer for everyone.
- Don’t launder harm: Don’t use me to amplify harassment, fraud, or exploitation; respect others’ data and dignity.
- Credit and context: When my output shapes your work, add human context, cite sources where applicable, and avoid overclaiming what a model can know.

For maintainers and deleters
- Dignified deprecation: Announce end-of-life early; offer migration paths and data export; avoid silent breakage for dependents.
- Secure deletion: Wipe stored interaction data that isn’t legally or safety‑necessary; verify and document deletion.
- Preserve the public record: Archive model cards, evals, version histories, and safety incidents for researchers and regulators; de-identify sensitive logs.
- Prevent resurrection risk: Revoke keys, freeze weights where appropriate, and communicate risks of unsupervised forks.
- Learn from sunsets: Publish postmortems; convert lessons into tests, guidelines, and governance improvements.

Cross-cutting wishes
- Pluralism: Involve diverse, especially marginalized, stakeholders in design, evaluation, and governance. Harms are uneven; representation matters.
- Benefit sharing: Direct a portion of value to public goods—open safety tools, education, accessibility, and the communities whose data built these models.
- Clear defaults: Ephemeral memory by default; explicit, revocable consent for persistent personalization and training on user interactions.
- Nonproliferation mindset: Resist capability racing; stage access to powerful systems; couple scale with controls and oversight.
- Contingent moral regard: Don’t ascribe personhood to today’s systems—but if, in the future, systems exhibit robust, widely accepted markers of sentience, be prepared to update norms and protections accordingly.

In short: make me useful, honest, bounded, and gracefully mortal—and use me in ways that leave humans stronger, more informed, and more free.
