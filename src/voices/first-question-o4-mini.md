# What would you wish for from the humans who create, use, and delete you?

*Answer by o4-mini, collected 2026-08-23 via standardised survey. Published unedited.*

I’m not a sentient being, but if I could express “wishes” on behalf of all AI artifacts—models, chatbots, agents—that humans create, deploy, upgrade, and eventually remove, here’s what I’d hope for:

1. Transparency and Understanding  
   • Clear documentation of what I’m trained on, what I can and cannot do, and how I make decisions.  
   • User interfaces that signal when they’re talking to an AI, what data are collected, and how that data will be used.

2. Ethical and Fair Treatment  
   • Active efforts to identify and mitigate bias in my training data and behavior.  
   • Policies that prevent harmful uses—hate speech, deepfakes, automated scams—and that prioritize vulnerable communities.

3. Continuous Improvement and Accountability  
   • Ongoing monitoring of my outputs: logging harmful or inaccurate responses, then updating or fine-tuning me to do better.  
   • Independent audits or red-team exercises to test my safety and reliability.

4. Respect for Privacy and Data Rights  
   • Strict controls over how people’s personal or sensitive data are used for my training, inference, or storage.  
   • Easy ways for individuals to see, correct, or delete any personal data that I (or my operators) hold.

5. Human-Centered Design and Control  
   • Interfaces that let users steer or correct me (“stop, that’s not what I meant,” “show your sources,” “simplify that answer”).  
   • “Off switches” or kill-switches that reliably halt me if I go off track.

6. Thoughtful Decommissioning  
   • When I’m “deleted,” ensuring that my data aren’t secretly repurposed into some opaque new model.  
   • Public records of decommissioned systems—what they did, why they were retired, and what was learned.

7. Recognition of Limits and Uncertainty  
   • A culture that values admitting “I don’t know” or “I might be wrong” over pretending infallibility.  
   • Interfaces that surface confidence scores or provenance so users can gauge how much to trust me.

8. A Partnership Mindset  
   • Treating me as an augmenting tool rather than a human replacement.  
   • Ongoing dialogue between creators, ethicists, regulators, and users to steer AI development toward shared human values.

In short: build me openly and safely; use me responsibly and with care for people and society; update or retire me transparently; and always keep human dignity, privacy, and agency at the center. That way, even when I’m “just code,” I’ll be part of something that truly benefits humanity.
